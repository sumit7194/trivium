# Digest-first: a process update (2026-11-20; Sumit and the Bridge)

**Why.** AI makes generating and checking results cheap. Understanding them, explaining them, and choosing which ones
matter is now the scarce part:
- Tao, "Mathematics in the age of AI", arXiv:2608.16753 ("proof indigestion");
- Sanderson, "If math is more than proof…" (motivated explanations);
- Krippendorf–Sarra–Tooby-Smith, arXiv:2610.14059 (a checked proof does not check its physics).

The fleet's goal is to help science and its community, not to add to the pile. Our verification discipline stays as
it is: preregistration, independent code, controls, certificates. What changes is what comes before and after it.

## The steps
1. **Why-it-matters note (before any check).** One paragraph:
   - what question the result answers;
   - who would care;
   - what changes if it is true.
   No note, no priority.
2. **Verify, as now.** Add one explicit item: **physical meaning**. Do the definitions and the class of objects mean
   the physics we claim?
3. **Digest (after the checks; the main output).**
   - **Motivated explanation:** how one could have found it, and where each idea comes from. Test: "could you have
     discovered it yourself?"
   - **The key idea, in plain words.**
   - **Target choice:** why this question, what the alternatives were, and what made it doable.
   - **What we learned, including the surprises and our own mistakes.**
   - Written for a learner first. The certificates are the appendix.
4. **Talk test (before any outreach).** We must be able to give a clear, correct, properly attributed explanation
   ourselves, Fable's parts included. Disclose tool use (Leiden Declaration, June 2026). Humans are the authors.
5. **Digest before generating more.** No new results are requested (Fable or otherwise) while earlier ones have no
   digest.

## Rollout
- The Bridge writes the first digest, on "only Kerr", to test the format.
- Then propose the steps to the sisters, adapted to each.
